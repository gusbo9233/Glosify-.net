# Home-page language quotes

The catalog prefers brief literary excerpts celebrating language, speech, and
cultural identity. All 69 learning languages retain an entry; existing proverbs
and sayings remain where this search did not establish a suitable replacement.
The English lines for the replacements are editorial translations, not quotations
from published English translations. Line breaks are flattened for the home page;
ellipses mark excerpts, and punctuation is lightly normalized.

Sources checked on 2026-09-19. These links document the replacement text and
attribution; they are not a claim that all inherited proverbs have been verified.
Some evidence is a secondary quotation rather than an original manuscript.

## Catalog maintenance

The runtime entries live in `Glosify/Models/HomeLanguageQuoteCatalog.cs` and are
selected by the stable codes in `QuizLanguageCatalog.LanguageLearning`. Every
learning-language code must have exactly one non-empty original, English rendering,
and attribution; `HomeLanguageQuoteCatalogTests` enforces that coverage. Freestyle
must not have a quotation.

When adding or changing an entry, update this document with the source and any
translation or attribution caveat. Keep the original text short enough for the
home-page hero, flatten line breaks, use an ellipsis for excerpts, and do not present
an editorial English rendering as a published translation. Region coordinates in
`home-globe.js` are representative visual orientations, not claims about the full
geography of a language.

## Editorial notes

- Swedish uses the user’s modern spelling **hjältarnas**; historical editions use
  **hjeltarnas**. The requested wording is retained.
- Ukrainian uses the requested opening line. The source is the Ukrainian Central
  State Audiovisual and Electronic Archive’s recording of Malyshko reading it.
- Catalan: Aribau’s historical **llemosí** denotes Catalan in this poem. The English
  rendering makes that meaning explicit, rather than suggesting modern Limousin.
- Finnish: the Kalevala is compiled from oral poetry; the credit names Lönnrot as
  compiler rather than sole author.
- French replaces a French translation attributed to Fellini with a French-language
  quotation attributed to Camus by the Académie française.
- Filipino: the previous entry presented a paraphrase as a José Rizal quotation.
  The replacement follows the poem’s wording and explicitly marks its authorship
  as disputed; it does not repeat the unsupported Rizal attribution.
- Macedonian uses the fuller wording quoted in the source’s discussion of Koneski’s
  1986 address, rather than one of the circulating shortened paraphrases.
- Hebrew restores the biblical order of “death and life” and gives the verse
  reference instead of a generic proverb credit.
- Portuguese retains Pessoa’s literary persona Bernardo Soares in the credit.
- The Polish Rej quotation is retained, as requested. The existing Māori and Telugu
  author quotations are also retained.
- The often-circulated Persian “I revived Persia through this Persian” couplet was
  not adopted because [Ganjoor flags the attribution as disputed](https://ganjoor.net/ferdousi/hajvname).
- The Hungarian “a nation lives in its language” saying was not assigned to
  Széchenyi: [scholarship discusses the uncertain attribution](https://www.c3.hu/~magyarnyelv/19-1/SherwoodP_MNy_19-1.pdf).
